# 2026-08-06 — English oak, drought scorch

**Subject:** English oak (front yard misc, on [Zone 1 — yard sprinklers](../PLAN.md#irrigation-zones))
**Photo:** [`../photos/2026-08-06-oak.jpg`](../photos/2026-08-06-oak.jpg)

## What I saw

Heavy leaf scorch across the upper and outer canopy — crispy tan/brown leaves worst on the exposed branches; interior lower foliage still green. Small mulch ring (barely past the trunk); turf grows right up to it on all sides. Hell-strip location (narrow planting strip between sidewalk and street) means reflected heat from asphalt and concrete on both sides. Late July / early August after a dry summer.

Prior related note: 2026-06-01 tip damage was attributed to late-frost damage from the 2026-05-05 hard freeze (see [`WEATHER.md`](../WEATHER.md) and the 2026-06-01 line in [`SCHEDULE.md`](../SCHEDULE.md)). The current pattern is different — this is drought/heat scorch on the whole canopy, not a single leader tip.

## Diagnosis

Drought and heat scorch on an unestablished young transplant. Symptoms fit: browning at leaf margins/tips, worst where transpiration is highest (upper/outer canopy), interior shaded foliage still green. Root causes at this site:

1. **Yard sprinklers alone are not enough** for a young oak's root zone — the Zone 1 run time is set for turf, which is a shallow-rooted crop, and doesn't drive water deep enough for a tree.
2. **Turf competition** — grass right up to the trunk wins the water fight.
3. **Reflected heat** from sidewalk + asphalt on both sides raises the effective evapotranspiration well above what a lawn setting would see.

Interior green means the cambium is alive. Recoverable if watered and mulched properly.

## Actions (planned + underway)

- **Deep soak weekly:** slow trickle at the drip line 45–60 min, or a soaker hose coiled around the root zone for ~2 hrs. Weekly through the rest of summer; taper in fall.
- **Expand mulch ring** to 3–4 ft diameter, 3–4" deep wood chips. Pull mulch back 1–2" from the trunk itself (no volcano).
- **Kill turf inside the ring by smothering:** flatten cardboard over the grass (overlap seams 4–6"), soak it, then cover with the wood chips. Do **not** dig, till, or spray herbicide near the roots — feeder roots are near the surface and easy to damage.
- **Don't prune the scorched wood yet.** Wait for 2027 spring leaf-out; a lot of what looks dead will still push buds. Only remove branches that are clearly snappable-dry with no green cambium under a scratch test, and only during dormancy.
- **Don't fertilize** — pushes leaf growth the stressed roots can't support.
- **Check stake ties:** if the root ball no longer rocks when the trunk is pushed, stakes should come off. Trunks need to flex to build strength; ties left on too long can girdle.

## Next check-ins

- **Late Aug – early Sept 2026:** Is new scorch expanding onto currently-green foliage? If yes, add a second deep soak per week.
- **Before ground freezes (~mid-Nov 2026):** One final deep soak going into dormancy.
- **Mid-winter (Jan–Feb 2027):** Winter-water once/month during dry spells with no snow cover on the ground (young Denver trees need this).
- **Spring 2027 leaf-out (Apr–May) — the decision point.** What leafs out tells us:
  - How much of the canopy survived → what (if any) dormant-season pruning is needed next winter.
  - Whether the tree is worth keeping — if more than ~50% of the canopy fails to leaf out, plan replacement (and choose a species better suited to a hell strip on the Front Range: bur oak, chinkapin oak, or Kentucky coffeetree hold up better than English oak in dry alkaline conditions).
